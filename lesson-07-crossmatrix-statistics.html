The Cross-Matrix, statistically: every cell is a regression you now understand

Six lessons of foundations, assembled into one CellEstimate.

Level US Graduate · applied econometrics / quant FR Master M2 · ENSAE 3A

The Cross-Matrix looks like a bespoke research engine, but statistically it's a grid of the exact objects from Lessons 1–6. Every cell fits one regime-conditional regression, stores its coefficient with a confidence interval, and earns trust through subsample robustness and shrinkage. Nothing in it is new machinery — it's your foundations, wired together and named for the desk.

2 · Why logs — the coefficient becomes an elasticity

Regressing ln Y on ln X makes the slope a unit-free elasticity: a percent move in X maps to a percent move in Y.

That's why the DAX-vol → SX5E-skew cell reads β ≈ +0.42: a 10% rise in DAX vol ≈ a 4.2% rise in the skew. But logs need Y > 0. When the target can be negative — a return, a beta, a signed skew — you switch to semi-elasticity (Y on ln X: ΔY per %ΔX) or delta-sensitivity (ΔY on ΔX). That is exactly the estimator choice your doc makes for the cash / delta-one cells versus the vol cells.

4 · What CellEstimate stores is inference (Lesson 1)

`point` is the estimate; `ci_low`, `ci_high`, `t_stat` are the frequentist inference around it — the interval and test statistic from Lesson 1. The standard error comes straight from the Lesson-5 variance:

One catch specific to your setup: the matrix aligns weekly / overlapping windows, so successive residuals are autocorrelated and that plain σ²(XᵀX)⁻¹ understates the true variance — the CIs would look falsely tight. That's the entire reason the doc wraps fits in Newey-West (HAC) standard errors: same β̂, honestly widened SEs. And the 95% CI keeps its Lesson-1 meaning — a statement about the procedure across resamples, not a 95% probability about this one interval.

5 · sign_stability & pooling — bias–variance in your pipeline (Lessons 2 & 5)

A stock-level cell has few observations in a given regime, so its β̂ is noisy — significant in-sample yet flipping sign across subperiods. Your doc's gatekeeper isn't the t-stat, it's sign_stability: the share of subperiods whose β̂ keeps the same sign. And bayesian_pooled_elasticity stabilises the estimate by shrinking it toward a sector/index prior — which is Lesson 2's estimator trade and Lesson 5's ridge, in one move:

6 · Regime conditioning = conditional expectation (Lesson 4)

β_r is the slope of the conditional relationship E[ ln Y | ln X, regime r ]. Fitting within a regime is conditioning the expectation on r — Lesson 4's object, sliced by state. A cell whose β changes across regimes is a conditional mean whose shape depends on the conditioning variable, which is the whole thesis of a regime matrix: the transmission is not a single number, it's a function of state.

7 · Your drivers are collinear (Lessons 5 & 6)

The driver panel includes dispersion at five horizons plus their z-scores, and several correlated country vols — highly collinear columns. So the design matrix's XᵀX is ill-conditioned, individual β's get inflated variance, and coefficients can swing sign sample to sample (Lesson 5's coefficient cloud). Two consequences your doc already encodes: don't over-trust any single driver's β when its neighbours are correlated — trust sign_stability; and pooling / regularisation is the stabiliser, exactly as ridge was for collinear features.

The precise statement

Each Cross-Matrix cell is an OLS estimate of a regime-conditional (semi-)elasticity: β̂_r = [(XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy] on the rows where the causal regime label is r, with HAC (Newey-West) standard errors because overlapping windows autocorrelate the residuals. Its stored CI and t-stat are Lesson-1 inference; Bayesian pooling is shrinkage toward a hierarchical prior (bias traded for variance); and sign_stability is a subsample-robustness diagnostic that the design uses in place of significance to separate structural cells from episodic noise.

The twist — clean statistics, fake alpha

Every estimator here can be textbook-correct and the cell still worthless, for a reason that isn't statistical at all: look-ahead leakage. If a regime label, a vol window, or a z-score peeks even one step into the future, β̂ fits beautifully in-sample and evaporates live — which is why your doc calls as-of discipline the "single biggest source of fake alpha" and enforces it at the primitive boundary (filtered, not smoothed, HMM state; trailing windows only). Two honest-signal habits fall out of the foundations: in-sample t-stats and R² overstate, so the real test is out-of-sample and cross-subperiod (that's what sign_stability measures); and the correct standard errors are the HAC ones, not the tight naïve ones. Rigour on the fit is necessary but never sufficient — the validity lives in the data discipline around it.

Reading your own heatmaps

  • A cell's number is β_r, the coefficient — not today's skew or vol level. "+0.42" means %ΔY per %ΔX in that regime, nothing about where Y sits now.
  • Check sign_stability before you believe a cell. A big β with low stability is noise dressed as signal — the design surfaces it but flags it; treat the diagnostic as the gate even though it no longer filters.
  • For the buildable-now cash / delta-one cells, the target can be negative, so they read as semi-elasticities (ΔY per %ΔX, signed) — don't interpret them as log-log elasticities.
  • When neighbouring drivers are correlated (the dispersion horizons, country vols), distrust any single β and lean on stability; that's collinearity, not signal.
  • Stock-level cells should be read pooled — the naïve single-name β is the noisy one; the shrunk estimate is the trustworthy cell.
