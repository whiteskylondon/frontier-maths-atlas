Lesson 15 · Wing III — probability & information

Entropy, cross-entropy & KL

Why almost every training loss is, underneath, a negative log-likelihood.

LevelUS Advanced undergrad → graduateFR Master M1–M2 · ENSAE 2A–3A

Information theory gives three quantities that run through all of modern ML. Entropy measures how uncertain a distribution is. Cross-entropy measures the cost of using a model distribution q to describe the true one p. And their difference, the KL divergence, is the penalty you pay for being wrong — always non-negative, zero only when the model is exact. Training a classifier by minimizing cross-entropy turns out to be identical to maximum likelihood, which is why "the loss is a negative log-likelihood" is not a slogan but a theorem.

Three quantities

They lock together in one identity — cross-entropy is the irreducible uncertainty of p plus the avoidable penalty for modelling it with q:

  • entropy — uncertainty in p. Maximal when all outcomes are equally likely; zero when one is certain. In nats (bits × ln 2).
  • cross-entropy — cost of coding p with q's code. The classification loss; minimized (at H(p)) exactly when q = p.
  • KL divergence — information lost using q for p. ≥ 0, asymmetric, zero iff identical. The model's excess surprise.

Why it's a negative log-likelihood

Set p to a single realized outcome (a one-hot) above, and cross-entropy collapses to −ln q(that outcome) — the log-loss of that single prediction, which is exactly the negative log-likelihood the model assigns to what happened. The same logic makes MSE a negative log-likelihood too — under a Gaussian noise model, −ln p(y|ŷ) = (y−ŷ)²/2σ² + const. Every standard loss is an NLL under some assumed noise distribution; that's why they yield calibrated probabilities and connect straight back to Lesson 3's MLE.

The precise statement

For distributions p, q on a finite set, H(p,q) = H(p) + D_KL(p‖q), and by Gibbs' inequality D_KL(p‖q) ≥ 0 with equality iff p = q — so cross-entropy is minimized over q exactly at q = p, where it equals H(p). Minimizing the empirical cross-entropy between the data distribution and a parametric model q_θ is equivalent to maximum-likelihood estimation of θ, i.e. minimizing D_KL(p_data‖q_θ). Under a Gaussian observation model the per-example NLL is (y−ŷ)²/(2σ²) + const, recovering squared-error loss; under a categorical model it is −ln q_θ(y), the log-loss.

The twist — KL has a direction, and it changes the answer

KL is not symmetric: D_KL(p‖q) ≠ D_KL(q‖p), and the two are minimized by different fits. Forward KL D(p‖q) — what maximum likelihood minimizes — is mode-covering: it punishes q for putting no mass where p has some, so q spreads to cover every outcome p can produce. Reverse KL D(q‖p) — what variational inference and the RLHF penalty (Lesson 35) use — is mode-seeking: q collapses onto one mode and ignores the rest. This is why a variational posterior looks too narrow, why a model trained by MLE hedges, and why the KL direction in a method is a design decision, not a detail. The deeper unification: entropy = optimal code length, cross-entropy = the code you actually paid, KL = the waste — and "minimize the loss" almost always means "minimize the KL from reality to your model," i.e. maximize likelihood. Once you see a loss as an NLL, its noise model, its calibration, and its failure modes all become legible.

Why this matters · volarixs

The entropy of a regime-probability vector is a clean uncertainty gauge: when your HMM's filtered state probabilities are spread out, H is high and the regime is genuinely ambiguous; when one state dominates, H → 0 and you're confidently in a regime. That number can gate how aggressively a regime-conditional signal fires.

For evaluating forecasts, log-loss is the honest score: a directional model that put probability q on the move that actually happened contributes −ln q, and averaging it is the model's NLL — lower is better, and unlike hit-rate it rewards calibrated confidence. And when you back out a risk-neutral density from option prices, the maximum-entropy density consistent with the quoted strikes is the least-committal one — the Buchen–Kelly construction is literally "assume nothing beyond the constraints," which is what entropy formalizes.
