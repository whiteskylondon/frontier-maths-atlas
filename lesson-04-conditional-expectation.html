Conditional expectation as a projection

The best predictor of Y from X, and why that sentence is geometry.

Level US Advanced undergrad → graduate FR M1–M2 · ENSAE 2A–3A

You run a single-stock book. Each day it returns Y; each day the index future returns X. You may short some amount of the future against the book, and the classic answer — the minimum-variance hedge ratio — is a single number, Cov(Y,X)/Var(X). This lesson is about what that number really is, and what it silently assumes. The best guess of Y once you have seen X is called the conditional expectation, written E[Y|X]. It is best in a geometric sense: treat random variables as vectors, mean-squared error as squared distance, and "everything you could build from X" as a subspace — then the best hedge is simply the closest point in that subspace, the orthogonal projection of Y onto it. A hedge ratio, a regression beta, a factor loading: each is the shadow of Y on some space of predictors. The only question is how big a space you let the shadow fall on.

The words, before any symbols
  • best guessE[Y|X] is the average of Y over just the days when X took today's value: feed in the index move, read out the book's expected move. It is not one number but a function of X — a curve. "Uses only the information in X" is the plain reading of "σ(X)-measurable" in the theorem box below.
  • hedge ratiothe h in Y − (a + hX): how many units of index future you short per unit of book. A hedge that is a straight line in X. The desk's h★ = Cov(Y,X)/Var(X) will fall out of the geometry, not out of a regression package.
  • projectionthe closest point to Y inside a chosen family of predictors — closest in mean-squared error, which is literally squared distance once you define length by E[U²]. Constants ⊂ straight lines ⊂ all functions of X: three nested families, three projections.
  • residualε = Y − (your predictor): the slippage the hedge leaves behind — the hedged PnL. Its variance is the number a hedging desk minimises. Under the best hedge, the residual is the part of Y that no function of X can touch.
  • orthogonaltwo random variables with E[UV] = 0 — "uncorrelated leftovers". The optimal residual is orthogonal to every candidate predictor: nothing usable remains. This one identity is the working definition of "best" in all of what follows.
The best guess, made formal

Everything happens in one geometry: the inner product ⟨U,V⟩ = E[UV] makes mean-squared error a squared distance, and the conditional expectation is defined as the minimiser over all functions of X:

  • the conditional expectation — a random variable, itself a function of X. The projection of Y onto L²(σ(X)), the space of square-integrable functions of X.
  • any candidate predictor built from X alone — line, curve, threshold rule, anything. Measurability is the fence: Z may not peek at information outside X.
  • the mean-squared error — hedge slippage variance in this lesson's units. Squared distance ‖Y−Z‖² under the inner product; minimising it is finding the closest point.

What certifies the winner is the orthogonality property: the residual of E[Y|X] is uncorrelated with every function of X — there is no predictable structure left for any hedge to use. Averaging both guesses gives the tower property:

  • the optimal residual — perpendicular to the whole subspace at once. Orthogonality to every g(X) is stronger than corr(ε,X)=0, and is exactly what the diagnostics below test bar by bar.
  • an arbitrary test function of the conditioning variable. Taking g ∈ {1, X, X², 1(X<0)} gives a practical residual test battery.
  • the tower property: refine your guess with information, then average — you recover the plain average. Set g = 1 in orthogonality. Iterated expectations is bookkeeping, not magic.

Pythagoras for this projection is the law of total variance — split Y into signal (the projection) plus noise (the residual), and squared lengths add:

  • variance of the signal — the part of the book's risk that some function of X could remove. The "explained" leg of the right triangle.
  • average leftover variance — the slippage floor no hedge built from X can go below. Idiosyncratic risk, in desk language; the noise σ² of the simulated world below.
The smaller subspace — the best straight line

A futures hedge cannot trade a curve: it is one ratio, a point in the two-dimensional subspace span{1, X}. Projecting Y onto that smaller plane instead of the full space gives the best linear predictor — and the normal equations (residual ⊥ 1, residual ⊥ X) solve it in closed form:

  • the minimum-variance hedge ratio — the projection coefficient of Y on X. In sample: Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ) / Σ(xᵢ−x̄)². Divide by n or n−1 (Lesson 2's story) — it cancels in the ratio.
  • the plane of affine hedges a + hX. Its residual is orthogonal to 1 and X only — to nothing else. That gap is this lesson's plot.
  • the intercept — centres the hedge so the residual has mean zero. Orthogonality to the constant function 1.

Because the subspaces nest — constants ⊂ span{1,X} ⊂ all functions of X — the slippage variances can only fall as the family grows. The whole lesson is the size of the last drop.

So far "conditional expectation" has meant "the MSE-optimal function of X". The honest definition runs the other way — Kolmogorov defines the object first, by measurability and matching integrals, and optimality becomes a theorem. Intuition: E[Y|X] is the unique X-readable variable that agrees with Y on average over every event that X can distinguish.

The precise statement

Let Y be integrable. The conditional expectation E[Y|X] is the almost-surely unique σ(X)-measurable, integrable random variable Z with E[Z·1_A] = E[Y·1_A] for every event A ∈ σ(X). If Y ∈ L², then E[Y|X] is the orthogonal projection of Y onto the closed subspace L²(σ(X)): it uniquely minimises E[(Y−Z)²] over σ(X)-measurable Z, and its residual satisfies E[(Y−E[Y|X])g(X)] = 0 for every bounded measurable g. Taking g ≡ 1 gives the tower property E[E[Y|X]] = E[Y]. Projecting instead onto the subspace span{1, X} yields the best linear predictor a★ + h★X with h★ = Cov(Y,X)/Var(X); the two projections coincide iff E[Y|X] is affine in X — in particular whenever (X,Y) is jointly Gaussian.

Interactive — where the slippage hides: moment diagnostics

Test the residual ε against four functions of X. The bars are sample moments E[ε·g(X)]; the shaded band is ±2 standard errors — inside it, a bar is indistinguishable from sampling noise. For the best straight line, ε ⊥ 1 and ε ⊥ X exactly (normal equations) — but nothing forces the X² or 1(X<0) bars to zero. For E[Y|X], orthogonality to all functions of X flattens the whole battery.

The twist — regression is only conditional expectation if the world is linear

Under joint Gaussianity, E[Y|X] is exactly a★ + h★X: the static futures hedge is the best hedge of any kind, and the Cov/Var ratio is the whole story. The moment returns grow asymmetric betas — and equity returns do — the two projections split, and the gap lands in specific moments: the linear residual stays uncorrelated with X while correlating with X², which is a short-gamma position you never chose — the book bleeds precisely when the index moves big. The tower property is the cruel part: E[ε] = 0 means the leak costs nothing on average, so a PnL mean tells you nothing is wrong — the bleed sits in the conditional tail and shows up in a crash, all at once. It is Lesson 1's marginal-versus-conditional distinction wearing a risk report: marginally flat, conditionally toxic.

Why this matters · volarixs

Every pairwise beta or correlation stored in a Cross-Matrix cell is exactly this construction: the L² projection of one asset's return on another, h★ = Cov/Var cell by cell. The whole matrix is a table of best-linear-predictor coefficients — and it inherits the blind spot on display above: a cell sees X, never X², so cross-asset convexity (asymmetric betas, crash co-movement) is invisible to it by construction.

The regime HMM is the other half of the lesson running in production: E[return | regime] weighted by filtered regime probabilities is the tower property — conditional means averaged over the conditioning partition recover the unconditional forecast. And the moment-battery above is the honest audit for any hedge the platform proposes: check ε against functions of X beyond X itself before calling a book flat.
