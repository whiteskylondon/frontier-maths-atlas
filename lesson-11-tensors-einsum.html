Tensors & the einsum world

Almost every operation in a neural network is one line of Einstein summation.

LevelUS Advanced undergrad → graduateFR Master M1 · ENSAE 2A

A tensor is just a multi-dimensional array — a scalar has no indices, a vector one, a matrix two, an attention score-block three or four. The single notation that unifies every operation on them is Einstein summation: write which indices each input carries, which the output keeps, and any index that appears on the left but not the right is summed over. Matmul, dot products, transposes, batched attention — all one einsum string. Learn to read the string and you can read any layer.

The precise statement

A tensor of order k is an element of a tensor product of k vector spaces, addressed by k indices. Einstein summation contracts an expression by summing over every index that is repeated across the operands and omitted from the result; the free (output) indices index the result's axes. Contraction generalizes the dot product (⟨u,v⟩ = Σᵢ uᵢvᵢ, "i,i->"), matrix product ("ij,jk->ik"), trace ("ii->"), and outer product ("i,j->ij"). It is associative and commutative in value but not in cost.

The twist — same value, wildly different cost

An einsum's result doesn't depend on the order you contract, but its FLOP count does — dramatically. Multiplying a chain A(1×1000)·B(1000×1000)·C(1000×1) left-to-right costs ~10⁹ operations; right-to-left costs ~2×10⁶. Choosing that order optimally is the classic matrix-chain problem, and it's why libraries have an optimize path and why FlashAttention is "just" a re-ordered, re-tiled version of the same attention einsum that never materializes the big score matrix. Two more traps worth naming: a tensor's order (how many indices) is unrelated to its rank in the SVD/decomposition sense (Lessons 8 and 12) — a 3-index tensor can be rank-1; and broadcasting is einsum with a silently repeated singleton axis. The notation is universal, but performance lives entirely in how you contract it.

Why this matters · volarixs

Your Cross-Matrix output is a tensor: the design doc names it (target, driver, regime, estimator) → CellEstimate — a 4-index object. Every heatmap slice, every "rank the drivers for this target in regime r," every aggregation across estimators is an index contraction or selection on that tensor. Thinking in einsum makes those slices one clean expression instead of nested loops.

And when you vectorize the regressions themselves — fitting many (target, driver) cells at once, or scoring a whole panel — the batched operation is a contraction like "btf,bfk->btk". The attention score you'll meet in Wing IV, "qd,kd->qk", is the same move: contract away the feature dimension, keep the pair. One language, from your matrix to the transformer.
