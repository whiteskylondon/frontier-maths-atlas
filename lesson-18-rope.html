Lesson 18 · Wing IV — inside the transformer

Rotary positional encodings (RoPE)

Attention can't see order. Rotate the queries and keys, and relative position falls out of the geometry.

Level US Graduate FR Master 2 (M2)

The attention scores of Lesson 17 have a blind spot: a dot product q·k knows what two tokens say, not where they sit — shuffle the sentence and every score survives the shuffle. Position has to be smuggled into the geometry. RoPE's move is the elegant one: don't add a position vector to the embedding — rotate each query and key by an angle proportional to its position. Rotations compose by adding angles, so the score between positions m and n ends up depending only on the gap n − m: relative position emerges from absolute rotations. A returns model wants exactly this invariance — what matters is how many days apart two observations sit (the lag structure), never which calendar dates they carry.

The words, before the machinery
  • blindpermutation-blind — attention's raw scores are dot products of content vectors, so reordering the tokens leaves every score intact. A feature for sets; a bug for language and for time series.
  • encodingpositional encoding — any scheme that stamps position onto the vectors so scores can notice order. The classic choice adds a learned or sinusoidal position vector; RoPE turns the vector instead.
  • rotationrotation — turning an arrow about the origin by an angle; its length never changes. Rotations are orthogonal maps — QᵀQ = I, the same Q Lesson 6's spectral theorem hands you — and one line finishes the story: ⟨Qx, Qy⟩ = xᵀQᵀQy = xᵀy, so inner products survive a rotation untouched.
  • phasephase — the angle a rotation has accumulated. Composing rotations adds phases: turn by α, then by β, is turn by α + β. This additivity is the whole trick — subtracting phases will manufacture the gap.
  • gaprelative position — the displacement n − m: five tokens back, three days of lag. The only positional quantity a translation-invariant score is allowed to depend on.
  • ladderfrequency ladder — d/2 rotation planes, each with its own speed θᵢ; fast planes resolve neighbours, slow planes carry long range. The same geometric spread of wavelengths you'd use to phase-encode seasonality in a returns model — one Fourier idea.
Rotate, don't add

Pair the d embedding coordinates into d/2 two-dimensional planes. A token at position m gets plane i turned by the angle m·θᵢ — an absolute rotation, one per plane, frequencies fixed once and for all:

  • frequency of plane i, geometric in i. base = 10,000 by default; i runs 0 … d/2−1, so θ falls from 1 down to base^(−1+2/d).
  • the position-m rotation — block-diagonal, one 2×2 rotation per plane. Orthogonal: R(m)ᵀR(m) = I, so ‖R(m)x‖ = ‖x‖ always (Lesson 6).
  • the 2×2 block: rotate plane coordinates by mθ. In einsum terms (Lesson 11) this is an elementwise map on the (seq × d) query and key arrays, applied just before the score contraction — zero parameters.

Why not simply add a position vector pₘ to the embedding? Expand the score: (q + pₘ)ᵀ(k + pₙ) = qᵀk + qᵀpₙ + pₘᵀk + pₘᵀpₙ — three leakage terms that each depend on an absolute position. Rotation multiplies instead of adding, and the absolute parts cancel by trigonometry:

  • query and key content at positions m and n. Per head, after the usual projections of Lesson 17.
  • the heart of it: R(m)ᵀR(n) = R(n−m). One-line proof: within each plane, rotations commute and angles subtract — Rθ(m)ᵀRθ(n) = Rθ(n−m); blocks act independently, so the whole product collapses.
  • the score depends on positions only through the gap. Shift both by s: (m+s, n+s) has the same gap, hence the same score — translation invariance for free.

The frequency ladder & the long-context knob

One plane aliases (hold that thought for the twist). The fix: run d/2 = 32 planes — here d = 64 — at geometrically spaced frequencies, so every plane sees position through its own wavelength:

  • wavelength of plane i — tokens per full revolution. A geometric ladder: at base 10⁴ with d = 64 it runs from 6.28 tokens up to λ₃₁ = 2π·10⁴^(31/32) ≈ 47,117. Mind the off-by-one-plane trap: the tempting "2π·base ≈ 62,832" overshoots, because the top plane is i = d/2 − 1, not i = d/2 — it lands at base^(−2/d) ≈ 75% of that figure, and the shortfall widens as the base grows (65% at base 10⁶).
  • the knob. Scaling base stretches every wavelength — the lever the long-context literature pulls. Fast (low-i) planes resolve near-neighbour order; slow (high-i) planes carry paragraph- and document-scale structure.

Below, top: the phase heat-strip — each cell is cos(m·θᵢ) across the 32 planes at position m; fast planes cycle, slow planes crawl. Bottom: the pure-geometry decay — a seeded random unit vector in ℝ⁶⁴ serves as both query and key content (k = q, isolating position from content), and we plot the 40-draw mean score between position 0 and position g. It is exactly 1 at g = 0 — unit vectors, zero rotation — and then falls away, oscillating, until it is rattling around the noise floor Lesson 10 set for ℝ⁶⁴, the ±1/√64 = ±0.125 scale at which two unrelated directions overlap: nearby positions correlate, distant ones carry no positional signal worth reading. It does not settle at zero, though. The curve is a finite sum of cosines, so it recurs — at base 10⁴ it climbs back to 0.48 near g ≈ 183,000 — and the twist explains why that is structure rather than a bug.

Re-applied — lag, not date

Point the same machinery at a returns panel and the property RoPE gives away for free is precisely the one you were going to have to assume anyway. A lead–lag score between two series is a claim about displacement — what a five-day lag does — and it must not care whether the window sits in March 2019 or October 2024. That indifference is ⟨R(m)q, R(n)k⟩ = ⟨q, R(n−m)k⟩, with the calendar index playing the token position. Bolt an absolute date vector onto the embedding instead and the additive expansion above leaks qᵀpdate and pdateᵀk into every score: the model can now learn "September" as a level effect, which is the textbook way to overfit a backtest.

The ladder re-appears too. No desk looks at one horizon; it looks at 1, 5, 21, 63, 252 days — a roughly geometric spread, chosen for the same reason RoPE spaces θᵢ geometrically. Short horizons resolve microstructure and are blind to regime turns; long horizons carry the regime and cannot see today; a geometric ladder covers the decades of scale in between at constant cost per rung. The heat strip above is that horizon panel, drawn as phase.

The precise statement

The precise statement

Fix any frequencies θ₀, …, θ_{d/2−1} and let R(m) be the block-diagonal d×d matrix whose i-th 2×2 block is the plane rotation by angle mθᵢ. Then each R(m) is orthogonal, so ‖R(m)x‖ = ‖x‖ for every x, and for all q, k ∈ ℝᵈ and all positions m, n: ⟨R(m)q, R(n)k⟩ = qᵀR(n−m)k. Attention scores computed from rotated queries and keys are therefore functions of the relative displacement n−m alone, invariant under every common shift m → m+s, n → n+s. Proof sketch: rotations of a fixed plane commute and their angles subtract, so each block satisfies Rθ(m)ᵀRθ(n) = Rθ(n−m); the blocks act on disjoint coordinate pairs, hence R(m)ᵀR(n) = R(n−m).

The twist — scores live on a torus

Under RoPE, position enters a score only through the d/2 phases (mθ₀, …, mθ_{d/2−1}) taken mod 2π — the score is a function on a torus, so it is almost-periodic in position. With a single plane the encoding genuinely aliases: positions m and m + 2π/θ are indistinguishable. What rescues it is the geometric ladder — incommensurate frequencies whose phase vectors don't realign for astronomically long stretches, the same trick as a Vernier scale (two mismatched rulers pinpointing a reading no single ruler could) or the Fourier basis reading a lag. And the relative-only property has a classical name: translation equivariance. RoPE hands the transformer the convolution's symmetry — scores depend on displacement, not location — without the convolution's fixed local kernel: attention still looks anywhere it likes, it just measures where in relative coordinates.

Why this matters · volarixs

Every Cross-Matrix cell (Lesson 7) is already keyed the RoPE way. A cell is indexed by (target, driver, regime) — never by calendar date — so its β is asserted to be a property of the relationship, not of when you happened to measure it. That is translation invariance, imposed by construction rather than learned. And sign_stability is the empirical audit of exactly that assumption: the share of subperiods whose β̂ keeps its sign is a test of whether the cell really is shift-invariant. A big β with weak sign_stability is a score that turned out to depend on absolute position after all — the additive-encoding failure mode, caught downstream instead of designed out.

The honest boundary: nothing in the platform runs RoPE, and bolting a rotary block onto the Cross-Matrix would be a solution hunting a problem. What transfers is the design discipline — decide which coordinate a score is allowed to depend on, then build that dependence into the parameterization instead of hoping the fit discovers it. RoPE hard-codes "gap only"; a regime-and-driver-indexed cell hard-codes "relationship only". Both make the invariance non-negotiable, which is why neither can be talked out of it by one lucky sample.
